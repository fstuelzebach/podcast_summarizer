**Executive Summary**

The US Treasury market remains resilient despite rising uncertainty and tariffs. Three reasons why: US policy credibility, collateral-based lending, and the utility of Treasuries as high-quality collateral. Markets have digested past tariff news and are calm, but there may be a disconnect between headline risk and market reaction.

The US economy is in good shape, with a large fiscal deficit and private sector balance sheets looking healthy. However, headwinds from tariffs and slower labor force growth create uncertainty that makes it difficult for businesses to plan. This can lead to lower economic growth in the long term, but not necessarily a crash.

Market positioning is not conducive to a trade that would take advantage of the dollar's weakness. The long-term outlook for the dollar remains bearish due to its weaponization and potential rate cuts by the Fed.

**Economic Mechanisms**

The US economy is supported by three key factors:

1. **Policy credibility**: The US has the most policy credibility around the world, making it difficult to lose confidence in Treasuries.
2. **Collateral-based lending**: The global financial system is collateral-based, with US Treasuries being the most pristine form of collateral. This makes it hard for people to lose confidence in Treasuries.
3. **Recursive effect**: When the private sector has more wealth, they invest part of that wealth, which increases demand for Treasuries. This recursive effect amplifies the demand for Treasuries.

The labor market shows signs of weakness, but this is not a typical recessionary pattern. The Atlanta Fed GDP forecast for Q3 remains strong, indicating that the US recovery is still intact. Uncertainty surrounding tariffs and potential politicization of the Fed will continue to impact investment decisions, leading to a desire to diversify away from USD and treasury bonds.

**Market Implications / Trade Ideas**

1. **Long Treasury Bonds**: The utility of Treasuries as high-quality collateral and their role in collateral-based lending make them attractive in times of uncertainty.
2. **Short-Dated Volatility**: As markets have digested past tariff news, short-dated volatility has fallen off a cliff. This could be an opportunity to go long volatility or buy options for low prices.
3. **Asset Allocation**: With the economy expected to grow at a lower rate in the long term, consider adjusting asset allocation towards more defensive sectors such as consumer staples and healthcare.
4. **Gold**: As uncertainty remains high, gold may continue to benefit from its safe-haven status.

Additional trade ideas:

1. **Long US Equities**: The current market environment is characterized by high equilibrium multiples, making it an attractive time to buy into the market.
2. **Short-Term Volatility Plays**: With financial institutions holding less risk and institutional investors positioned similarly, short-term volatility plays may not generate significant returns.
3. **Long-Term Trend-Following Strategies**: The current market environment favors long-term trend-following strategies that capitalize on structural growth trends in the US economy.

Note: These trade ideas are based on the assumption that the market will continue to be driven by behavioral factors and institutional investor positioning, rather than macroeconomic indicators.
