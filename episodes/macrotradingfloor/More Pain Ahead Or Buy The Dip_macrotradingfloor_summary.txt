**Economic Mechanisms (Japan)**

The recent trend of quantitative easing and fiscal stimulus by governments has been a significant factor in boosting economic growth. By printing money and increasing spending, policymakers can stimulate aggregate demand and boost asset prices. This strategy is particularly effective during times of economic uncertainty or when voters are incentivized to support politicians who promise economic growth.

One key mechanism underlying this phenomenon is the concept of monetary policy transmission. Central banks can use quantitative easing to inject liquidity into the financial system, reducing interest rates and making borrowing cheaper. This, in turn, can stimulate investment and consumption, ultimately boosting economic growth.

Another key mechanism underlying this phenomenon is the concept of voter incentives. When politicians can print money and increase spending, they create a "weapon" that incentivizes voters to support them. This can lead to a perverse incentive structure, where policymakers prioritize short-term gains over long-term sustainability, potentially undermining democracy.
